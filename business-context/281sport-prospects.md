# 281Sport — the addressable market, named

Built 2026-08-09 from ASOIF and ARISF membership rosters. This is the list the
`/acq` diagnosis said had never been made.

**Correction to the diagnosis:** I estimated a tight ICP of 300–800 organisations.
That was too loose. The top tier — international federations — is **about 71
organisations worldwide, and they are all named below.** The larger number only
appears once you add continental confederations and NGBs, which are tier 2 and 3.

Seventy-one is not a market you advertise into. It is a market you contact.

---

## The finding that changes the approach

**WAKO is an ARISF member.** Your existing client sits inside a 40-member
association whose members meet at a General Assembly and at SportAccord.

That means this is not a cold outreach problem. It is a **referral problem with a
warm anchor already inside the room.** One introduction from WAKO to the ARISF
secretariat or to two or three peer federations is worth more than any list I can
build, and costs nothing.

Work that first. Everything below is what you do in parallel, not instead.

## Tier 2 — Continental confederations (~150–250, unenumerated)

Each significant IF has continental bodies (Europe/Asia/Africa/Americas/Oceania).
Examples: UEFA, CAF, FIBA Europe, World Rugby regional unions, European
federations for each ARISF sport.

**Not yet enumerated.** Build these per-IF once tier 1 is worked — they are
smaller, less resourced, and *more* likely to need outsourced design, so they may
convert better than tier 1 even though the retainer is smaller.

## Tier 3 — National governing bodies (thousands)

Deprioritise for now. Budgets are smaller, the count is unmanageable without
filtering, and tier 1 has not been touched. Ireland alone has ~60 NGBs if a local
proof-of-concept is ever wanted.

## Contacts to find (Clay)

Two roles per organisation, in priority order:

1. **Communications / Marketing Director** — owns the output queue, feels the
   pain weekly, usually has budget discretion for outsourced production.
2. **Secretary General** — signs, but delegates the evaluation. Wrong first
   contact unless the federation is small.

For federations under ~15 staff the Secretary General *is* the comms lead. Check
staff size before choosing.

## Sequence

1. **This week — WAKO and ITA referral asks.** Referral-shaped, not a pitch: who
   else has this problem? Name ARISF explicitly. Free, fastest, highest yield.
2. **This week — enrich the 40 ARISF names** with Clay: website, staff size,
   comms lead, email. Skip any with an obvious in-house studio.
3. **Weeks 2–4 — outreach to ARISF 40**, personalised per federation using their
   *actual* published output as the hook. Reference WAKO by name — a peer inside
   their own association is the strongest proof you have.
4. **Then ASOIF 30**, filtering out the ones with large comms departments.
5. **Then tier 2**, built per-IF.

## Numbers to hold yourself to

At 71 tier-1 organisations, ordinary B2B response rates for a well-researched
niche with a named peer client, plus the WAKO referral path:

- 71 contacted → 10–20 replies → 5–8 conversations → **2–4 clients**

That is the whole top tier. It is a few weeks of work, not a campaign budget.
**If the retainer is EUR 2,000/month, 3 clients is EUR 72k a year from a list
that fits on one page.**

Replace that retainer figure with the real one — it is still `NOT TRACKED` and it
is the number that decides whether this tier alone is the business or just the
start of it.
