# Sales — speed, rubrics, and team structure

Reconstructed from public write-ups of Acquisition.com's Scaling Workshop
material. Use when the constraint is **sales** — leads arrive and don't convert.

---

## Speed to lead

The single highest-leverage sales fix in most businesses.

**Target: contact within 60 seconds** of a lead applying without booking.

**Why:** likelihood of conversion drops from roughly **70% → 20%** if a lead
leaves without booking. Nothing else in the sales process recovers that.

### The "free-for-all" setting process
- Leads who apply but don't book become **open** — first setter to claim them wins
- Claim = double dial + text
- Lead stays on the list for **7 days** provided it's contacted daily
- Use a **dummy internal calendar** with extended booking windows so setters can
  always offer a slot

---

## The closer rubric

Objective grading of recorded calls against **31 specific components**.

- Score each component: *not quite* / *good* / *great*
- **Give feedback on the single lowest-scoring component only.** One correction
  per review. Multiple corrections produce zero change.
- Maintain a separate **off-call rubric** covering health, mindset, CRM hygiene,
  and admin
- Grading can be delegated to a QC VA — this frees the sales manager's bandwidth,
  which is usually the real constraint on a sales team's improvement

**Why rubrics beat coaching by feel:** they make performance diagnosable,
delegable, and comparable across reps, and they remove the manager's mood from
the feedback.

## Operationalising abstract behaviour

Never train an abstraction. Unbundle it into observable, gradeable actions.

**Worked example — "active listening"** becomes three checkable behaviours:
1. Head nodding while the prospect speaks
2. Verbal affirmations ("ah", "ooh", "mm")
3. Mirroring — reciting their information back in fewer words

All three required to meet standard. Now it can be taught in onboarding
roleplays, graded on a rubric, and improved. Apply this unbundling to every soft
skill you expect from a team.

## Incentives — the three amplifiers

Any reward is amplified by delivering it:

1. **Randomly** — unpredictability (slot-machine effect) strengthens the behaviour
   far more than a predictable payout
2. **Quickly** — the tighter the pairing with the behaviour, the stronger the link
3. **Publicly** — status gain multiplies the value of the reward at no extra cost

### Weekly competitions beat monthly
- Motivation stays live across all seven days rather than spiking at month end
- Weekends get incentivised
- A bad week resets instead of writing off a whole month
- Structure: tiered cash bonuses **plus** random non-cash rewards

Other competitions worth running: CRM accuracy, daily closing rate (reduces
follow-up load), random pipeline-activity spot bonuses.

### Team-wide praise protocol
Create a notification-enabled channel where the whole company celebrates every
new client immediately. This publicly reinforces the single most important
behaviour in the business, continuously, for free.

## Scaling the team — the player-coach model

At 3+ closers or setters:

- Promote the top closer to **Closer Lead**, managing 3+ reps
- Promote the top setter to **Setter Lead**, managing 3+ reps
- **Time split: 80% revenue-generating work, 20% coaching and training**
- **Compensation: base role income + a percentage of the pod's performance**

This keeps your best performer selling (you don't lose their production) while
multiplying their method across a pod, and it aligns their pay with the pod's
output.

## Behaviour change: modeling then roleplay

Two pathways that actually change behaviour:

1. **Modeling** — the learner watches an expert execute
2. **Roleplay** — the learner practices with the expert guiding

Applied to sales: watch the top closer take real calls (modeling), then roleplay
with them (roleplay). Reading a script does neither and changes nothing.

---

## Application checklist

- [ ] What is my actual median speed to lead, measured in seconds?
- [ ] Do unbooked applicants get claimed and worked, or do they die in the CRM?
- [ ] Do I have an objective rubric, or do I coach on vibes?
- [ ] Am I giving one correction per review, or ten?
- [ ] Are rewards delivered randomly, quickly, and publicly?
- [ ] Weekly competitions or monthly?
- [ ] At 3+ reps, do I have a player-coach with an 80/20 split and pod comp?
- [ ] Is every soft skill I expect unbundled into gradeable behaviours?
