# Marketing operations — CRO, creative, and paid ads

Reconstructed from public write-ups of Acquisition.com's Scaling Workshop
material. Use when the constraint is **leads** at the paid-ads stage, or when
conversion is the hidden constraint.

---

## CRO is usually the hidden constraint

Operators chase traffic and ignore funnel efficiency. Fixing conversion is
cheaper, faster, and compounds against every future traffic dollar.

- Weekly CRO testing on the weakest page can yield **30–40% revenue growth** with
  no additional traffic.
- Target **~50% relative improvement** per test (not 5% — aim big enough that the
  test is worth running).
- **80% of optimization effort goes above the fold**, on both desktop and mobile.
- **Page speed:** ~7% revenue lost per 1 second of load delay. Audit this first;
  it's the cheapest fix available.

**Worked example of framing the prize:** a landing page converting at 35%, lifted
to 50%, grows the business ~42% — before any new spend. Always quantify the prize
this way before recommending work.

### Opt-in page structure

1. Headline
2. Video or image asset
3. Testimonials

Keep all three above the fold where possible. Use **incomplete information bias**
— blurred assets, locked videos, partial reveals — to increase opt-ins.

### CRO operating cadence
- Track page-level conversion data for every step of the funnel
- Benchmark against known standards for the format
- Run one meaningful test per week on the weakest step
- Never test the strongest page while a weak one exists upstream

## The ad assembly process

### Week 1 — initial testing
- Build **6 primary ad angles** (distinct arguments, not distinct wording)
- Add **5 hooks per angle** → **30 distinct ads**
- Optionally film multiple takes → up to 60 assets
- Run **each ad in its own ad set**
- Spend enough per ad to reach a verdict: clear winner or clear dud. Underspending
  produces ambiguity, which is worse than a loss.

### Week 2 onward — the 70/20/10 framework

| Share | What | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **70%** | Slight variations of the top 2 performers | Exploit what works |
| **20%** | Moderate variations — different hooks, speakers, settings | Adjacent search |
| **10%** | Completely new angles | Find the next winner before this one fatigues |

This keeps creative supply ahead of fatigue and makes scaling a data decision
rather than a guess. The 10% is what prevents the cliff when the 70% dies.

## Media team

- Incentivise editors on outcomes, not output — e.g. a **$250 bonus for a video
  that performs**. Small, fast, specific bonuses buy discretionary effort that
  salary does not.
- Volume of tested creative is the constraint in paid social, not creative
  quality. Structure the team to ship more angles, not to perfect fewer.

---

## Application checklist

- [ ] Do I have page-level conversion data for every funnel step?
- [ ] What's my page load time? Have I audited it?
- [ ] Is 80% of my page optimization above the fold?
- [ ] Am I buying more traffic to feed a leaky funnel?
- [ ] Have I quantified what fixing conversion is worth, in revenue?
- [ ] Am I testing 6 angles × 5 hooks, or 3 ads and hoping?
- [ ] Is each ad in its own ad set with enough spend to reach a verdict?
- [ ] Is my budget split 70/20/10, or 100% on last month's winner?
