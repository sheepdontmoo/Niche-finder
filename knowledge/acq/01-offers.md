# Offers — the Grand Slam Offer

Source frameworks: *$100M Offers*. Use when the constraint is **sales** (leads
arrive but don't convert) or when the offer is commoditised and competing on price.

---

## The Value Equation

```
                Dream Outcome  ×  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
VALUE  =  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                Time Delay  ×  Effort & Sacrifice
```

Four levers. Increase the top two, decrease the bottom two. Most offers only ever
push "Dream Outcome" — the leverage is usually in the denominator.

| Lever | Direction | How to move it |
|---|---|---|
| **Dream Outcome** | ↑ | Sell the end state, not the mechanism. Not "a gym" — "lose 20lbs." |
| **Perceived Likelihood** | ↑ | Proof, case studies, guarantees, specificity, reversal of risk |
| **Time Delay** | ↓ | Faster first win. Deliver *something* immediately, even if partial |
| **Effort & Sacrifice** | ↓ | Do it for them. Remove steps, decisions, learning curve |

**Diagnostic use:** when an offer isn't converting, ask which of the four is
weakest in the *prospect's perception* — not in reality. Perception is the term
in the equation.

## Building the offer — 5 steps

1. **Identify the dream outcome.** What the customer actually wants, in their words.
2. **List every obstacle** they face before, during and after buying. Be exhaustive
   — this list is the raw material. Aim for 20+.
3. **Convert each obstacle into a solution.** One-to-one mapping. Every obstacle
   becomes a component of the offer.
4. **Define delivery vehicles** using the *Delivery Cube*:
   - **Ratio:** one-to-one / small group / one-to-many
   - **Client effort required:** high / medium / low (lower is more valuable)
   - **Medium:** in person / phone / email / text / software / physical
   - **Format:** live / recorded / done-with-you / done-for-you
5. **Consolidate.** Trim components that cost a lot and deliver little value;
   keep and stack the ones that are high-value and low-cost to deliver. The
   winning combination is *high value to them, low cost to you*.

## The five enhancers

Applied after the core offer exists. These convert a good offer into one that
sells dramatically better.

1. **Scarcity** — limited supply. Real caps on cohort size, slots, inventory.
2. **Urgency** — limited time. Deadlines, cohort start dates, expiring pricing.
3. **Bonuses** — stack additional wins that address specific objections. Each
   bonus should kill one named hesitation. Assign each a dollar value.
4. **Guarantees** — the single biggest price lever. Bigger guarantee → bigger
   price you can charge. Types: unconditional refund, conditional ("do the work
   and don't get X, we refund"), anti-guarantee, implied performance.
5. **Naming** — see MAGIC below.

## MAGIC naming formula

| Letter | Element | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **M** | Magnet | Why does this offer exist right now? |
| **A** | Avatar | Who is this for, specifically? |
| **G** | Goal | What's the dream outcome? |
| **I** | Interval | How long will it take? |
| **C** | Container | What *kind* of thing is it? |

Container words: Blueprint, Masterclass, Challenge, Experience, Cohort,
Accelerator, Bootcamp, Intensive, Solution, Template, System, Method, Program.

Example shape: *"The [Magnet] [Avatar] [Goal] in [Interval] [Container]"* →
"The Post-Holiday Contractor Booked-Solid 30-Day Accelerator."

## Pricing tactics

Three approaches that work without changing the product:

1. **Announce increases with a transition date.** Existing clients get the old
   price until date X; new clients pay more. Creates urgency without discounting.
2. **Anchor high, then discount.** Establish the full price, then present the
   actual price as a reduction from it.
3. **Pre-payment discounts for loyal clients.** Converts future revenue into
   present cash — directly improves FECC:CAC and payback period.

**Principle: charge for outcomes, not access.** Access is a commodity; outcomes
are not.

## Application checklist

- [ ] Can I state the dream outcome in the customer's own words?
- [ ] Have I listed 20+ obstacles and solved each one in the offer?
- [ ] Which Value Equation term is weakest? Have I attacked *that* one?
- [ ] Does every bonus kill a specific, named objection?
- [ ] Is the guarantee big enough to be uncomfortable? (If it's comfortable, it's too small.)
- [ ] Does the name pass MAGIC?
- [ ] Is this offer high-value to them and low-cost to me?
