# Money Models — offer sequencing and cash

Source frameworks: *$100M Money Models*. Use when the constraint is **margin/cash**
— growing but cash-poor, or unable to fund acquisition.

---

## The central goal

> **Recoup the cost of acquiring a customer within 30 days.**

If you get CAC back in gross profit within 30 days, you can use existing cash or
credit to acquire a customer, be repaid inside the month, and reinvest the same
dollar to acquire the next one. Growth stops being a budget question.

This is why money models beat marketing tactics: a business with a 30-day payback
and a mediocre ad can outspend — and therefore outgrow — a business with a great
ad and a 9-month payback.

---

## The four offer types

A money model is these four combined into one **sequence**, not four separate
products.

### Stage I — Attraction Offers
Turn strangers into first-time customers. Purpose is *acquisition*, not profit.
Lower the barrier to entry and start the relationship. Judged on volume of
customers acquired and cash collected, not on margin.

### Stage II — Upsell Offers
Maximise revenue from a customer you've already paid to acquire. The moment after
purchase is the highest-intent moment you will ever have with them. Upsells are
where the acquisition cost actually gets paid back.

### Stage II — Downsell Offers
The safety net. When a prospect says no to the primary offer, present a modified
or lower-priced alternative. Converts a dead lead — already paid for — into
revenue. Every "no" without a downsell is a fully-funded loss.

### Stage III — Continuity Offers
Recurring revenue. Predictable cash that isn't dependent on constantly finding
new customers. This is what turns a business from a treadmill into an asset, and
what makes LTGP large enough to satisfy LTGP:CAC > 3.

## The metrics that govern it

| Metric | Definition | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **CAC** | All sales + marketing cost ÷ new customers | As low as the model allows |
| **Gross Profit** | Revenue − cost to deliver | Reason in this, never in revenue |
| **LTGP** | Lifetime **gross profit** per customer | — |
| **FECC** | Front-end cash collected (~first 30 days) | — |
| **LTGP : CAC** | Return on acquisition | **> 3** |
| **FECC : CAC** | Ability to self-fund growth | **> 2** |
| **Payback period** | Time to recoup CAC in GP | **≤ 30 days** |

**Always gross profit, never revenue.** A $10,000 sale at 20% margin funds less
acquisition than a $3,000 sale at 80%. Revenue-based reasoning is how profitable-
looking businesses run out of money.

---

## Designing the sequence

1. **Start from the constraint.** Failing FECC:CAC → fix the front end (attraction
   offer, price, payment terms, upsell at point of sale). Failing LTGP:CAC → fix
   the back end (continuity, retention, expansion).
2. **Attraction offer**: what's the lowest-friction way to convert a stranger into
   a paying customer *today*?
3. **Immediate upsell**: what do they need next, offered at the moment of highest
   intent — right after they buy?
4. **Downsell**: what's the version for someone who wants it but said no?
5. **Continuity**: what recurring need does the outcome create? Attach it.
6. **Recompute** payback and both ratios. Iterate until payback ≤ 30 days.

---

## Cash-flow tactics that need no new customers

These improve FECC and payback immediately:

- **Pre-payment discounts** — convert future revenue into present cash
- **Annual billing at a discount** vs monthly (also reduces churn)
- **Deposits / setup fees** on delivery-heavy offers
- **Price increases with a transition date** — see `01-offers.md`
- **Upsell at point of sale** rather than later, when intent has decayed

## Application checklist

- [ ] Do I know CAC including labour, not just ad spend?
- [ ] Am I reasoning in gross profit rather than revenue?
- [ ] What is my payback period, in days, right now?
- [ ] Is LTGP:CAC > 3? Is FECC:CAC > 2?
- [ ] Do I have all four offer types, or only an attraction offer?
- [ ] Is there an upsell at the point of sale, not weeks later?
- [ ] Does every "no" hit a downsell?
- [ ] Is there any continuity attached to the outcome?
