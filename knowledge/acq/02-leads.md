# Leads — the Core Four

Source frameworks: *$100M Leads*. Use when the constraint is **leads** — and only
after confirming conversion isn't the real problem (see `00-diagnosis.md`).

---

## There are only four ways to get a lead

| # | Channel | Who you contact | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Warm outreach** | People who already know you | 1-to-1, free, immediate |
| 2 | **Posting free content** | People who follow you | 1-to-many, free, compounding |
| 3 | **Cold outreach** | Strangers | 1-to-1, paid (time/labour), scalable |
| 4 | **Paid ads** | Strangers | 1-to-many, paid, most scalable, riskiest |

**Sequencing rule:** start warm → add content → add cold outreach → add paid ads
*only once the first three run on their own*. Paid ads amplify whatever is
already true: a working funnel scales, a broken one burns cash faster.

---

## Channel notes

### 1. Warm outreach
Contact people who already know you. **Do not pitch directly.** Ask who *else*
they know who might benefit. Referral-shaped asks preserve the relationship and
multiply reach. This is the fastest zero-cost path to first revenue and is
routinely skipped because it's uncomfortable.

### 2. Posting free content
Maximise **value per second** of consumption. Publish where the avatar already
is. Content compounds — the audience persists after the post stops working.
Consistency beats production quality.

### 3. Cold outreach
Three requirements, all mandatory:
- **Volume** — the number must be big enough to survive the response rate
- **Personalisation** — enough specificity that it reads as sent to one person
- **Persistence** — multiple touchpoints; one attempt is not an attempt

### 4. Paid ads
Most scalable, least forgiving. Requires a validated offer and a funnel with
known conversion rates. See `04-marketing-ops.md` for the ad assembly process
and the 70/20/10 budget framework.

## Lead magnets

A lead magnet is given free in exchange for contact information. **It should be
good enough that people would have paid for it.** A weak lead magnet produces
leads that don't convert — worse than no leads, because it wastes sales capacity.

Seven types:
1. Software or tools
2. Information (guide, report, training)
3. Done-for-you service sample
4. Free trial
5. Physical product
6. Community access
7. Event access

**Design rule:** the lead magnet should solve a *narrow* problem completely,
revealing the *broader* problem your paid offer solves. Solving the broad problem
for free removes the reason to buy; solving nothing removes the reason to trust.

## Scaling any channel: More, Better, New

Applied in order — this is the correct sequence when a channel plateaus:

1. **More** — increase volume of what already works. Usually the answer. Most
   "the channel stopped working" problems are actually "we never scaled it."
2. **Better** — improve conversion at each step of what you're already doing.
3. **New** — add a new angle, format, or channel. Last resort, not first instinct.

Operators habitually jump to **New** because it's the most interesting and the
least uncomfortable. It's almost always the wrong move while **More** is untapped.

## Application checklist

- [ ] Have I confirmed leads — not conversion — is actually the constraint?
- [ ] Which of the Core Four am I running, honestly, at real volume?
- [ ] Am I skipping warm outreach because it's uncomfortable?
- [ ] Is my lead magnet good enough that someone would pay for it?
- [ ] Have I fully exhausted **More** before reaching for **New**?
- [ ] Do I know my cost per lead *and* cost per customer by channel?
