# People and scaling

Reconstructed from public Leila Hormozi / Acquisition.com material. Use when the
constraint is **people/ops** — the owner is the bottleneck, quality varies, or
growth stalls because delivery can't absorb it.

---

## Hiring

**Hire for traits, teach the skills.** The guiding principle, borrowed from the
Ritz-Carlton: *"We don't hire people who know how to make beds. We hire people
that are good people."*

- Prioritise emotional intelligence and character over technical skill, especially
  below senior level. Technical skill is teachable on a known timeline; character
  is not.
- The **interview is mutual evaluation** and is already the beginning of
  onboarding — candidates are forming their model of how the company operates
  from the first contact.
- Weak hiring filters are one of the recurring root causes across portfolio
  companies, alongside unclear KPIs and inconsistent onboarding.

## Operational clarity

The core operating thesis: **build systems that produce consistent outcomes
regardless of who is doing the work.**

Three requirements:
1. **Defined roles** — everyone knows what they own
2. **Measurable outcomes** — every role has a number
3. **Repeatable systems** — hiring, onboarding, and performance review are
   processes, not events

The payoff is reduced decision fatigue, reduced dependency on any single
contributor, and the ability to scale headcount without a proportional explosion
in complexity.

**The three recurring failures** to check for in any business:
- Unclear KPIs
- Weak hiring filters
- Inconsistent onboarding

## Retention

Three incentives that keep people, in rough order of power:

1. **Mentorship** — they're visibly getting better at something they care about
2. **Identity recognition** — being seen and named for what they contribute
3. **Leadership opportunities** — a path to more scope

Delivered **quickly, randomly, and publicly** (see `05-sales.md` — the same three
amplifiers apply to all reinforcement).

### Young talent
**Overinvest in life coaching; undercompensate financially.** Young high-potential
people optimise for growth and attention over marginal salary.

Weekly 1:1s should cover:
- Personal development *outside* the current role
- Their future ambitions and the skills those require
- How the current role builds toward those skills

Someone who feels holistically supported doesn't leave for a 10% raise.

## Status as a retention mechanic

People don't leave places where they've established status. Build a visible
status ladder:

- Title-based progression tied to tenure and results
- Virtual or physical status markers — badges, swag, access
- Exclusive access unlocked at achievement milestones
- **Critical requirement:** a clear, published roadmap showing the path to the
  next level. Status without a visible path is just hierarchy, and demotivates.

(This is the same mechanic Vantage uses on members via revenue verification —
see `../../research/acq-vantage-teardown.md`. It works internally and externally.)

## Referral and affiliate incentives

Rewards must be **large enough to change behaviour**. Standard ~20% commissions
generate minimal engagement because they don't justify the discretionary effort.

Thought experiment to run on any incentive: *if I tripled this, would effort
increase significantly?* If yes, the current number is too small to be doing
anything, and you're paying for nothing.

## Experience design

The most-remembered moments of any experience are the **beginning and the end**.
People forget the middle content and remember the framing and the emotional
conclusion.

- **Opening:** warm welcome with hyper-specific compliments
- **Closing:** an unexpected bonus reveal or gift

Applies to events, onboarding, client delivery, and offboarding.

## Matching advice to stage

| Stage | People focus |
|---|---|
| **$0–1M** | Owner does everything. Don't hire yet. |
| **$1–3M** | First hires remove the owner from delivery, not from selling. |
| **$3–10M** | Build and empower a team; owner moves from doing to managing. |
| **$10–100M** | Subject-matter experts per department; generalists stop working. |

Hiring ahead of the stage burns cash; hiring behind it caps growth. The tell for
"behind" is that quality varies by who did the work.

## Application checklist

- [ ] Am I hiring for traits or for résumé skills?
- [ ] Does every role have a defined owner and a number?
- [ ] Is onboarding a documented process or a different experience every time?
- [ ] Are my three recurring failures (KPIs, filters, onboarding) actually solved?
- [ ] Is recognition delivered quickly, randomly, and publicly?
- [ ] Is there a published path to the next status level?
- [ ] Are my referral incentives big enough to change behaviour, or theatre?
- [ ] Does my headcount match my stage?
